How we score.

The evaluation criteria for the Internship Challenge. Exactly what we look at, and how much each
part counts.

The one question behind all of it:  what shows you can safely own part of a real product?

T H E   P R I N C I P L E
What we're scoring

We care about how you think, not how impressive your app looks. Making something look good is easy now.
What's hard, and what we're actually looking for, is evidence that you understand what you built, checked that it
works, and could own it as real software.

The percentages below are a guide, not a strict formula. We form a first view from your submission, the code,
the live app, your docs, and your demo. If you're shortlisted, the pitch is where we dig into your understanding
and reasoning, which is the biggest slice.

The criteria

30%   Understanding & reasoning
The biggest factor. Can you explain how your app works, why you built it that way, the trade-offs you weighed,
and the goal it serves along with its limits. This is mostly what we dig into at the pitch.

W H A T   E A R N S   M A R K S

Explaining your choices and the alternatives you considered, knowing where the app falls short, and showing
where something you learned changed your approach.

20%   Correctness & verification
Evidence that the core workflow actually does what it should, and how you checked it. We care more that you
verified it than that it is flawless.

W H A T   E A R N S   M A R K S

Testing or otherwise checking the core flow, catching your own issues, and being honest about what you did
and didn't verify.

20%   Engineering judgement & responsible AI use
How well you worked, especially with AI. You may use AI freely, and we don't count how much you wrote by
hand. What matters is that you delegated the right work, verified the output, rejected what was wrong, and
avoided complexity you didn't need. You own everything you submit, including the AI-assisted parts.

W H A T   E A R N S   M A R K S

Thoughtful AI use you can explain, simple and appropriate solutions, and code that someone new could pick
up and contribute to.

15%   Product
Does the core workflow work and solve the problem you chose, for the person who would actually use it.
Usability matters. Feature count, visual polish, deployment, and extra platforms do not.

W H A T   E A R N S   M A R K S

One complete, usable workflow that genuinely solves the problem, over a broader app that does more but
finishes less.

15%   Code quality
Clean, readable, reasonably structured code, with the complex logic documented. We're looking for good habits,
not perfection.

W H A T   E A R N S   M A R K S

Clear structure and naming, and a short note on anything non-obvious so a reader can follow it.

D O N ' T   C H A S E   T H E S E
What doesn't earn points

Chasing these won't lift your score, and it usually costs you time you'd spend better elsewhere:

• Feature volume. More features is not more impressive. It is usually less.

• Visual polish. A beautiful UI on top of a workflow you can't explain counts for nothing.

• Deployment and extra platforms. Nice to have, but not scored.

• Elaborate AI setups. A fancy agent pipeline earns nothing if you can't explain it. Simple tools used well beat

it.

• Hours spent. Time is not a score. A focused 10 hours beats a padded 40.

G O O D   T O   K N O W
A note on commit history

We look at your commit history as supporting context, not a scored item. Please prefer small, meaningful
commits over one final bulk commit. They don't need to be perfect, and we don't assess commit count. A clear
history simply helps us follow the purpose and progression of your changes. If you're new to Git, that's fine, and
it won't count against you.
